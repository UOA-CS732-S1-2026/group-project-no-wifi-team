# Story Events

## 1. Story Concept

The game tells the story of an international student living through one academic year.  
The year is divided into four quarters. Each quarter has a different emotional and practical focus.

The player chooses planned events from three categories:

- Study
- Entertainment
- Social

After planning, one random life event is added to the quarter.

The purpose is not to make every choice clearly good or bad.  
Each choice should involve trade-offs. For example:

- Studying improves Intelligence, but may reduce Health.
- Entertainment improves Health, but may reduce Wealth or Intelligence.
- Social events may improve Health, Intelligence, or Wealth, but can also cost time and energy.
- Random events make life feel unpredictable.

## 2. Game Flow Story

### Start

The player chooses a character.  
Each character has different starting strengths and weaknesses.

Example character types:

| Character | Main Strength | Main Weakness |
|---|---|---|
| Academic Achiever | High Intelligence | Low Health |
| Rich Kid | High Wealth | Low Intelligence or Health |
| Fitness Enthusiast | High Health | Low Wealth |
| Ordinary Student | Balanced attributes | No strong advantage |
| Heavenly Dragon | Strong Intelligence and Wealth | Very low Health, or special high-risk route |

### Quarter Planning

At the start of each quarter, the player sees the current quarter theme and chooses any 3 planned events from Study, Entertainment, and Social categories.

The player can focus on one area or balance their life.

### Event Story Scene

Each event is shown as a short story scene.  
The player only has two choices:

- Participate
- Skip

After choosing, the player sees the attribute changes and a short result sentence.

### Quarter Summary

After four events, the quarter summary shows:

- What happened this quarter
- Which choices the player made
- Attribute changes
- Current attribute levels
- A short narrative summary

### Ending

After Quarter 4, the final attributes determine the ending.  
The ending is saved into the ending gallery.

## 3. Quarter Narrative Themes

| Quarter | Theme | Story Feeling |
|---|---|---|
| Quarter 1 | Orientation & Settling In | New environment, adaptation, basic life setup |
| Quarter 2 | Midterm Pressure | Study pressure, deadlines, routines, money pressure |
| Quarter 3 | Expansion & Complications | Social life, group work, friends, trips, flat issues |
| Quarter 4 | Final Sprint & Future Path | Final exams, health, career, future direction |

## 4. Quarter 1 Story: Orientation & Settling In

The player has just arrived in a new country.  
Everything feels unfamiliar: the campus, public transport, local shops, flatmates, and the university system.

### Quarter 1 Opening Text

> You have just started your international student life. The campus is new, the city feels unfamiliar, and even simple things take more effort than expected. This quarter is about finding your rhythm and building your first routine.

### Quarter 1 Planned Event Stories

#### Campus Orientation Tour

You join a campus tour to learn where important places are.  
You find the library, classrooms, student services, and quiet study areas.

- Participate: You feel more confident navigating campus.
- Skip: You save time now, but later you feel confused and less prepared.

#### First Week Study Plan

You sit down and plan your first week of study.  
You check deadlines, lecture times, and readings.

- Participate: Your study life becomes more organized.
- Skip: You feel relaxed now, but your tasks become harder to track.

#### Library Card Setup

You activate your library account and learn how to use research databases.  
It is not exciting, but it makes future assignments easier.

- Participate: You gain useful academic resources.
- Skip: You may struggle later when assignments require sources.

#### Explore the City

You go out and explore the city centre.  
The trip helps you feel less trapped in your room.

- Participate: Your mood improves, but you spend some money.
- Skip: You save money, but the city still feels unfamiliar.

#### Cooking Starter Challenge

You try to cook a proper meal instead of ordering takeaway.  
The result may not be perfect, but it is part of becoming independent.

- Participate: You feel healthier and save money.
- Skip: Takeaway is easy, but it slowly drains money and health.

#### Room Decoration

You buy a few small items to make your room more comfortable.  
A small plant, poster, or lamp can make the room feel less lonely.

- Participate: Your living space feels more like home.
- Skip: You save money, but your room still feels temporary.

#### Orientation Party

You attend a new student party and meet other international students.  
The conversations are awkward at first, but you realize many people feel the same.

- Participate: You feel more connected.
- Skip: You avoid discomfort, but may feel more isolated.

#### Join a Campus Club

You join a club based on your interests.  
It gives you a reason to leave your room and meet people regularly.

- Participate: You build social connections and learn about campus life.
- Skip: You avoid extra commitment, but miss an easy way to make friends.

#### Flatmate Introduction

You talk to your flatmates and try to build a friendly living environment.  
A short conversation can reduce future tension.

- Participate: The flat feels more comfortable.
- Skip: You avoid awkwardness, but the flat remains distant.

### Quarter 1 Random Event Stories

#### Lost on Campus

You get lost on campus and cannot find the right building.

- Participate: You ask someone for help and learn the route.
- Skip: You wander around alone and become stressed.

#### SIM Card Problem

Your SIM card does not work properly, making navigation and communication difficult.

- Participate: You spend time and money fixing it.
- Skip: You delay the problem and miss useful messages.

#### First Grocery Shopping

You go grocery shopping for the first time.

- Participate: You buy useful food and supplies.
- Skip: You rely on expensive or unhealthy quick meals.

#### Homesickness

You suddenly miss home and feel emotionally low.

- Participate: You call family or do something comforting.
- Skip: You ignore the feeling, but it grows heavier.

## 5. Quarter 2 Story: Midterm Pressure

The semester becomes serious.  
Deadlines and exams arrive, and the player starts to feel the pressure of balancing study, sleep, money, and daily life.

### Quarter 2 Opening Text

> The easy beginning is over. Midterms are coming, assignments are piling up, and daily routines are harder to maintain. This quarter tests whether you can manage pressure without losing yourself.

### Quarter 2 Planned Event Stories

#### Midterm Revision Plan

You prepare a proper study plan for midterms.

- Participate: Your academic confidence improves, but it costs energy.
- Skip: You feel less stressed for now, but academic risk increases.

#### Attend Office Hours

You visit the lecturer or tutor to ask questions.

- Participate: You understand difficult topics more clearly.
- Skip: You avoid effort, but confusion remains.

#### Assignment Sprint

A deadline is close, and you decide to focus intensely on the assignment.

- Participate: You make strong progress, but feel exhausted.
- Skip: You rest now, but the assignment becomes a bigger problem later.

#### Movie Night After Class

You watch a movie to relax after a long study day.

- Participate: Your mood improves, but study time and money are reduced.
- Skip: You stay productive, but feel more tense.

#### Gym Stress Relief

You exercise to release exam pressure.

- Participate: Your body and mood improve.
- Skip: You save time, but stress builds up.

#### Sleep Recovery Day

You take a day to recover from poor sleep.

- Participate: Your health improves, but study progress slows.
- Skip: You push forward, but tiredness gets worse.

#### Study Group Before Midterm

You join classmates for revision.

- Participate: You learn faster, but lose some rest time.
- Skip: You avoid pressure, but miss group support.

#### Flatmate Dinner

You cook or eat with your flatmates.

- Participate: You feel less alone and may save money.
- Skip: You keep to yourself and feel more disconnected.

#### Help a Classmate

A classmate asks for help with course content.

- Participate: Explaining helps you learn, but uses energy.
- Skip: You save time, but lose a chance to build goodwill.

### Quarter 2 Random Event Stories

#### Library All-nighter

A deadline is close, and you consider staying overnight in the library.

- Participate: You catch up academically, but your health drops sharply.
- Skip: You sleep properly, but lose academic progress.

#### Bad Weather Week

It rains constantly and affects your routine.

- Participate: You adjust your plan and keep going.
- Skip: You let the bad weather ruin your week.

#### Part-time Shift Offer

Your manager offers an extra shift.

- Participate: You earn money, but lose energy and study time.
- Skip: You rest and study, but miss income.

#### Takeaway Temptation

You feel too tired to cook and want to order takeaway repeatedly.

- Participate: It is convenient, but hurts health and wealth.
- Skip: Cooking takes effort, but saves money and improves health.

## 6. Quarter 3 Story: Expansion & Complications

The player has become more familiar with student life.  
However, more opportunities and more problems appear: friends, group work, trips, networking, flat conflicts, and money decisions.

### Quarter 3 Opening Text

> You are no longer completely new, but life has become more complicated. Friends invite you out, assignments require teamwork, and small life problems become harder to ignore.

### Quarter 3 Planned Event Stories

#### Group Assignment Leader

Your group needs someone to organize the assignment.

- Participate: You gain leadership experience, but the stress is heavy.
- Skip: You avoid pressure, but the group may become disorganized.

#### Extra Tutoring Session

You attend extra tutoring.

- Participate: Your learning improves, but it costs money.
- Skip: You save money and rest, but academic progress slows.

#### Online Course Night

You study an online skill at night.

- Participate: You improve yourself, but lose sleep.
- Skip: You rest, but miss a chance to grow.

#### Weekend Trip

Friends invite you on a weekend trip.

- Participate: You recover mentally, but spend money.
- Skip: You save money and time, but miss a memorable experience.

#### Eat Out With Friends

You eat out with friends.

- Participate: Your mood improves, but your wallet suffers.
- Skip: You save money, but lose a social moment.

#### Gaming Night

You play games with friends at night.

- Participate: You relax and bond with friends, but lose study focus.
- Skip: You stay productive, but miss fun.

#### Friend's Birthday Party

A friend invites you to a birthday party.

- Participate: Your friendship improves, but it costs money.
- Skip: You save money, but the friendship may feel distant.

#### Networking Event

You attend an alumni or industry event.

- Participate: You gain future opportunities, but it is tiring.
- Skip: You rest, but miss potential career connections.

#### Cultural Festival

You join a cultural festival.

- Participate: You feel proud and connected.
- Skip: You avoid effort, but miss a meaningful campus event.

### Quarter 3 Random Event Stories

#### Phone Breaks

Your phone breaks unexpectedly.

- Participate: You pay to fix it.
- Skip: You keep using a broken phone and lose study efficiency.

#### Cheap Grocery Deal

A supermarket has a useful discount.

- Participate: You save money and prepare better meals.
- Skip: You miss a chance to improve daily life.

#### Flatmate Conflict

A conflict happens in the flat.

- Participate: You try to solve it, but the conversation is stressful.
- Skip: You avoid conflict, but tension remains.

#### Second-hand Bargain

You find a useful second-hand item online.

- Participate: You improve daily life cheaply.
- Skip: You miss the deal.

## 7. Quarter 4 Story: Final Sprint & Future Path

The last quarter decides the final direction.  
The player faces final exams, health risks, career chances, and the emotional end of the year.

### Quarter 4 Opening Text

> The final quarter has arrived. Every choice feels heavier now. You need to finish the year, protect your health, and think about what comes next.

### Quarter 4 Planned Event Stories

#### Final Exam Sprint

You enter full exam revision mode.

- Participate: Academic performance improves strongly, but health drops.
- Skip: You protect your health, but final results may suffer.

#### Academic Workshop

You attend a workshop on writing or presentation skills.

- Participate: You gain useful academic skills.
- Skip: You save energy, but miss practical support.

#### Portfolio Polish

You organize projects, assignments, and learning evidence.

- Participate: Your future opportunities improve, but it takes time.
- Skip: You rest, but your career preparation is weaker.

#### Sleep Recovery Day

You take time to sleep and recover.

- Participate: Your health improves strongly.
- Skip: You push through tiredness and risk burnout.

#### End-of-Semester Treat

You reward yourself for surviving the year.

- Participate: You feel happier, but spend money.
- Skip: You save money, but feel emotionally drained.

#### Long Walk Reset

You take a long walk to clear your mind.

- Participate: Your health and focus improve.
- Skip: You stay inside and remain stressed.

#### Internship Networking Event

You attend a career networking event.

- Participate: You gain future opportunities, but it costs energy.
- Skip: You rest, but miss career progress.

#### Farewell Dinner

You eat with friends before the semester ends.

- Participate: You create a warm memory, but spend money.
- Skip: You save money, but the ending feels lonely.

#### Ask Senior for Advice

You ask senior students for advice about courses and internships.

- Participate: You gain useful information.
- Skip: You avoid the conversation, but miss guidance.

### Quarter 4 Random Event Stories

#### Scholarship Application

You find a scholarship opportunity.

- Participate: You prepare the application and may improve future finance.
- Skip: You avoid stress but lose a potential chance.

#### Flu Season

Many students are getting sick before finals.

- Participate: You protect your health carefully.
- Skip: You ignore it and risk getting sick.

#### Career Email Reply

You receive an important career-related email.

- Participate: You reply carefully and protect a future opportunity.
- Skip: You delay it and lose momentum.

#### Final Week Burnout

You feel close to burning out.

- Participate: You slow down and recover.
- Skip: You keep forcing yourself and health drops sharply.

## 8. Writing Style Notes

The story should feel realistic and student-like.  
It should not sound too dramatic all the time.  
Small everyday problems can still matter because they affect the player's long-term attributes.

Good event style:

- Short and clear
- Realistic for international student life
- Has a trade-off
- Connects to attributes naturally
- Helps the final ending feel earned
